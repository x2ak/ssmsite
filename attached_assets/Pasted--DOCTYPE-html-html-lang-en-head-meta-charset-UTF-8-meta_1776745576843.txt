  <h2><span class="h2-num">01</span> What Happened</h2>

  <div class="incident-card">
    <div class="card-label">⚠ Incident Origin</div>
    <p>
      The breach originated from <strong>Context.ai</strong>, a third-party AI tool in use by a Vercel employee. The attacker leveraged compromised OAuth credentials from that tool to gain access to the employee's Google Workspace account — and from there, to Vercel's internal environment variable store. Only environment variables <strong>not</strong> marked as 'sensitive' were accessible. No npm packages published by Vercel were tampered with, and Vercel's core infrastructure remained operational.
    </p>
  </div>

  <p>
    This was not a brute-force or opportunistic attack. Vercel's own security team described the attacker as <strong>highly sophisticated</strong>, citing their operational velocity and precise understanding of Vercel's internal architecture. This type of third-party OAuth pivot is an increasingly common vector — and exactly the sort of threat we monitor for on behalf of our clients.
  </p>

  <!-- How we found out -->
  <h2><span class="h2-num">02</span> How We Found Out — Before Vercel Confirmed It</h2>

  <p>

When credible indicators of this incident surfaced on monitoring channels, our team was already aware of the situation <strong>hours before Vercel's public confirmation</strong>.
  </p>
